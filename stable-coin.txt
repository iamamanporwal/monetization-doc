 01. ONE-PAGE SUMMARY
================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |
  |                                                                          |
  | A stablecoin is a digital dollar. One USDC is always worth about one US  |
  | dollar. People can send it from a phone app to anyone in the world in   |
  | seconds, for less than one cent.                                         |
  |                                                                          |
  | We want to add "Pay with USDC" next to "Pay with Card" and "Pay with    |
  | UPI" in HERE. The customer pays in digital dollars, a payment company   |
  | (Stripe) instantly turns them into normal dollars, and normal dollars   |
  | land in our bank. We never hold crypto. The customer gets credits,      |
  | exactly as if they had paid by card.                                    |
  |                                                                          |
  | Why bother? It costs us 1.5% per payment instead of ~6% (Paddle) or     |
  | ~4% (cards) on a $39 plan. It lets people without good cards pay us.    |
  | And it prepares HERE for a future where AI agents pay for work by       |
  | themselves.                                                              |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+

  THE RECOMMENDATION IN ONE TABLE

  +---------------------------+----------------------------------------------+
  | Question                  | Answer                                       |
  +---------------------------+----------------------------------------------+
  | Should we do this?        | Yes, as an ADDITIONAL payment method, not a  |
  |                           | replacement for cards / UPI.                 |
  | What coin?                | USDC only at launch.                         |
  | What blockchains?         | Solana + Base first, Polygon allowed,        |
  |                           | Ethereum allowed with a fee warning.         |
  | Hold crypto?              | No. Convert 100% to dollars automatically.   |
  | Build or buy?             | Buy. Use a payment gateway (Stripe first).   |
  | Who is it for?            | Builders, creators, AI-native users, and     |
  |                           | users whose cards fail or cost extra.        |
  | How do we attract users?  | Bonus credits (not a lower price).           |
  | What does it earn us?     | ~$0.35-$1.37 more margin per Prime payment   |
  |                           | even AFTER paying the bonus (Section 15).    |
  | What blocks us?           | Which legal entity bills customers           |
  |                           | (US entity = simple; Indian = complex).      |
  | When?                     | 6 phases over ~90 days (Section 20).         |
  +---------------------------+----------------------------------------------+

  THE ONE RULE THAT OUTRANKS EVERYTHING (inherited from the Money Model)

    Nobody is ever charged for something they did not knowingly start,
    and nobody is ever charged for something that failed.
    Paying in USDC must be exactly as safe and predictable as paying by card.

 02. PLAIN-ENGLISH GLOSSARY
================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |
  | Crypto has a lot of jargon. Here is every word used in this document,    |
  | explained with an everyday comparison.                                   |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+

  STABLECOIN
    A digital token designed to always be worth one unit of a normal
    currency (usually $1). Think of it as a "digital dollar bill" that lives
    on the internet instead of in your pocket.

  USDC
    The stablecoin issued by Circle, a US company. Backed 1:1 by cash and
    short-term US government bonds. Regulated in the US and EU. The "safe,
    boring, compliant" digital dollar. THIS IS THE ONE WE WILL ACCEPT.

  USDT (Tether)
    The biggest stablecoin, issued by Tether (offshore). Very popular in
    emerging markets, but has regulatory problems in the EU and US.

  BLOCKCHAIN / CHAIN / NETWORK
    The public "road" the digital dollar travels on. Examples: Ethereum,
    Solana, Base, Polygon, Tron. Same coin, different roads - some roads
    have expensive tolls, some are almost free.

  WALLET
    The app where a person keeps their digital dollars. Examples: MetaMask,
    Phantom, Coinbase Wallet. Like a banking app, but the person holds the
    keys themselves.

  SELF-CUSTODY
    Holding your own keys (passwords) to your wallet. If you lose them, the
    money is gone - no bank can reset it.

  GAS / NETWORK FEE
    The toll paid to use the blockchain road. On Solana or Base it is less
    than one cent. On Ethereum it can be $2 to $20.

  PAYMENT GATEWAY / PROCESSOR
    A company (Stripe, Coinbase Commerce, CoinGate) that sits between the
    customer's wallet and our bank. It watches the blockchain, confirms the
    payment arrived, and converts it to normal money. Like a card terminal,
    but for digital dollars.

  ON-RAMP / OFF-RAMP
    On-ramp = turning normal money into crypto. Off-ramp = turning crypto
    back into normal money that can go to a bank account.

  FIAT
    Normal government money: US dollars, rupees, euros.

  SETTLEMENT
    The moment the money is final and belongs to us. Card payments settle in
    2-3 days. Stablecoin payments settle in seconds.

  FINALITY
    When a blockchain payment can no longer be reversed. On Base: about 2
    seconds "soft", ~15 minutes "hard". On Solana: about a second.

  CHARGEBACK
    When a card customer asks their bank to take the money back. The
    merchant loses the money plus a fee. Blockchain payments have NO
    chargebacks - once sent, they cannot be pulled back by the sender.

  MERCHANT OF RECORD (MoR)
    The company legally selling to the customer. Paddle is an MoR: it
    handles global sales tax for us. Stripe is NOT an MoR: we handle tax.

  KYC / KYB
    "Know Your Customer / Know Your Business" - identity checks required
    by law.

  AML
    Anti-Money-Laundering rules. Includes screening wallets against
    sanctions lists.

  DEPEG
    When a stablecoin briefly stops being worth exactly $1 (e.g. $0.97).
    Rare for USDC, but it has happened (March 2023, for a few days).

  FX / FX SPREAD
    Foreign exchange. The spread is the hidden cost when one currency is
    converted to another (the difference between the "real" rate and the
    rate you actually get).

  BASIS POINT (bps)
    One hundredth of a percent. 100 bps = 1%. Used to quote small fees.

  DEFERRED REVENUE
    Money a customer paid us for service we have not delivered yet. It is a
    debt we owe in service, not profit yet. Becomes revenue when credits
    are used.

  BREAKAGE
    Credits bought but never used before they expire.

  VDA (India)
    "Virtual Digital Asset" - India's legal name for crypto, including
    stablecoins. Taxed at 30% on gains + 1% TDS on transfers.

  TDS (India)
    Tax Deducted at Source - 1% withheld on crypto transfers under
    Section 194S.

  GENIUS ACT (US)
    The US federal stablecoin law signed July 2025. Regulates who can issue
    stablecoins. Takes effect 18 Jan 2027 at the latest.

  MiCA (EU)
    The EU's crypto law. It is why USDT disappeared from many EU checkouts.

  x402 / MPP
    New protocols that let AI agents pay for things automatically over the
    internet using stablecoins. Early stage.

  TOKENIZED DEPOSIT
    A bank's own version of a digital dollar (e.g. JPMorgan's JPMD). Like a
    stablecoin, but issued by a bank. Institutional only for now.

 03. THE PROBLEM WE ARE SOLVING
================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |
  | Today every payment into HERE loses a chunk to fees, some customers     |
  | can't pay at all, and small top-ups are unprofitable. Stablecoins fix    |
  | parts of all three.                                                      |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+

  PROBLEM 1 - Card and Paddle fees eat margin, especially on small payments

    Paddle charges ~5% + $0.50. Cards via Stripe charge ~2.9% + $0.30.
    The fixed part ($0.30 / $0.50) is what kills small payments.

      Fee as % of a $10 top-up
      Paddle  |##########################################| 10.0%
      Card    |########################                  |  5.9%
      USDC    |######                                    |  1.5%
              0%                                       10%

    That is why the Money Model sets a $10 minimum top-up. With USDC the
    fee has NO fixed part, so even a $2 top-up keeps 98.5% of its value.

  PROBLEM 2 - Some customers cannot pay us easily

    - Many creators, freelancers and builders outside the US/EU have cards
      that fail on international SaaS, or pay 3%+ foreign transaction fees.
    - A growing group of people are PAID in USDC (by Deel, Meta, freelance
      platforms) and would rather spend it directly than convert it.

  PROBLEM 3 - The future customer may be an AI agent

    HERE runs Guardians and agents. Cards were built for humans typing
    numbers into forms. Stablecoins can be paid by software, 24/7, in tiny
    amounts. This is early, but it is where the product is heading.

  WHAT WE ARE NOT TRYING TO SOLVE

    - We are NOT trying to become a crypto company.
    - We are NOT trying to replace cards or UPI.
    - We are NOT letting anyone cash credits OUT as crypto (that would make
      us a payments/money business - see Section 18).

================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |
  | Headlines say stablecoins move trillions. Almost all of that is traders  |
  | moving money around. REAL payments are much smaller - but growing fast,  |
  | and most of it is businesses paying businesses across borders.          |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+

  4.1  THE HEADLINE vs THE REALITY

    Gross stablecoin transfers in 2025 ............ $28 - 62 TRILLION
    Of which: genuine real-economy payments ....... ~$350 - 550 BILLION
                                                    (BCG, McKinsey, BIS)

       Imagine a stadium of 100 people moving money.
       Only ~1 of them is actually buying something.
       The other 99 are traders, bots and exchanges shuffling funds.

  4.2  THE MOST RECENT HARD NUMBERS (Alvarez & Marsal, 29 Sep 2026)

    - Stablecoin supply at end of August 2026: $303 billion (+6% YoY).
    - Stablecoin PAYMENTS, first 8 months of 2026: at least $401 billion,
      up 42% year over year.
        --> Usage is growing 7x faster than supply. People are USING
            digital dollars more, not just holding more.
    - Businesses receive an estimated 58-64% of that payment volume.
    - B2B settlement alone: $137-153 billion.
    - Drivers named: remittances, service fees, payroll, supplier payments.

  4.4  WHERE CHECKOUT ACCEPTANCE ACTUALLY LIVES

    StablecoinSpend (Q3 2026) found stablecoin checkout is concentrated in
    hosting, VPS, domains, VPNs, proxies, dev tools. Physical retail is
    almost absent. They explain why - and every reason applies to HERE:

      [x] Buyers are technical and already hold crypto   --> HERE: builders
      [x] Small recurring bills where card fees hurt     --> HERE: $39 plans,
                                                             top-ups
      [x] Instant delivery, so disputes are rare         --> HERE: credits
                                                             arrive instantly
      [x] Some buyers prefer not to use a card           --> HERE: global
                                                             creators

    CONCLUSION: HERE is the kind of business where stablecoin checkout
    actually works. A supermarket is not.

  4.5  WHAT PAYMENT PROCESSORS SEE (CoinGate, H1 2026, 782,403 payments)

    - USDC became the #1 payment asset at 22.1% (overtaking Bitcoin at 21.0%).
      USDC share over three half-years: 9.3% -> 16.4% -> 22.1%
    - Merchants converted 75.4% of payments straight to fiat.
      Share kept as crypto: 39.3% -> 33.4% -> 24.6%  (falling every half)
    - USDC was 88.1% of all payouts; 93.2% of payouts were automated by API.
    - Average order ~95 EUR, falling while order count rises
      (= more frequent, smaller, everyday use).

  4.6  THE BIG PLAYERS (who builds the roads)

    - Stripe: bought Bridge (~$1.1B). Stablecoin checkout on by default since
      Sept 2025, 1.5% fee, settles to your Stripe balance in local currency.
    - Tempo: Stripe + Paradigm's own payments blockchain. Mainnet live
      18 March 2026. Fees paid in stablecoins, no gas token needed.
    - Visa: stablecoin settlement at ~$7B annualized, 130+ card programs.
    - Rain: stablecoin card issuer, valued at $1.95B (Jan 2026).
    - Coinbase: x402 protocol for AI-agent payments.
    - JPMorgan: JPMD tokenized deposit live on Base for institutions.

  4.7  THE HONEST FORECAST (Deloitte, May 2026)

    - Stablecoins could support ~2.5% of US non-cash transactions by 2030,
      mostly BEHIND THE SCENES (settlement, funding), not at the checkout.
    - >$200B of US retail payments "stablecoin-enabled" by 2030.
    - Early adoption led by CARDS (stablecoin-funded Visa/Mastercard cards),
      then AI-agent commerce, then merchant loyalty programs.

    WHAT THIS MEANS FOR US: stablecoin checkout will be a MINORITY of our
    payments for years. We are building it for (a) margin on the payments
    that do come through, (b) access for underserved users, (c) readiness
    for agent payments - not because it will replace cards.

================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |

  | swaps digital dollars for normal dollars. The bank receives it.          |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+

  +-----------+    +-------------+    +--------------+    +------------+    +----------+
   Customer         Public "road"      Watches the road,   Converts the     Money is
   signs once,      that moves the     matches payment     digital dollar   usable.

   (<1 cent on      IRREVERSIBLE.      screens for         money.           a liability

  +--------------+---------------------------+--------------------------------+

 06. DECISION 1: HOLD CRYPTO OR CONVERT TO DOLLARS?
================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |
  | When a customer pays in USDC, we can keep it as USDC or immediately swap |
  | it into regular dollars. Our bills (AI models, servers, guardians) are   |
  | in dollars. Keeping USDC adds risk and paperwork for no benefit.         |
  | So: convert everything, automatically, every time.                       |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+

  OPTIONS

  +----------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------+
  | Option               | Good                    | Bad                     |
  +----------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------+
  | A. Convert 100%      | No crypto on our books. | Pays a small conversion |
  |    automatically     | Same accounting as card.| (included in 1.5% on    |
  |                      | No depeg risk. Simple   | Stripe).                |
  |                      | for auditors/investors. |                         |
  +----------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------+
  | B. Hold 100% USDC    | Can pay suppliers/      | Depeg risk, custody     |
  |                      | creators in USDC        | risk, crypto accounting,|
  |                      | without re-converting.  | India tax nightmare,    |
  |                      |                         | scares banks/investors. |
  +----------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------+
  | C. Split: convert by | Best of both            | Needs a written treasury|
  |    default + small   |                         | policy and discipline.  |
  |    USDC float        |                         |                         |
  +----------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------+

  WHAT THE MARKET DOES: 75.4% of CoinGate merchant payments convert to fiat,
  and the share keeping crypto has fallen three halves in a row.

  DECISION
    Phase 1-5 : Option A. Convert 100%.
    Phase 6+  : Option C, ONLY if we start paying Guardians / creators /
                suppliers in USDC. Float capped at 30 days of expected USDC
                payouts (see Section 17).

 08. DECISION 3: WHICH BLOCKCHAIN?
================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |
  | The same USDC can travel on different "roads". Some roads charge $5+    |
  | per trip (Ethereum). Some charge a fraction of a cent (Solana, Base).    |
  | On a $39 purchase, a $5 toll is insulting. We steer people to cheap,     |
  | fast, well-supported roads.                                              |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+

  8.1  COMPARISON

  +-----------+-------------------+----------------+-------------+------------+
  | Chain     | Typical USDC fee  | Speed          | Wallets     | Verdict    |
  +-----------+-------------------+----------------+-------------+------------+
  | Solana    | ~$0.0003 - $0.01  | ~1 sec (300ms  | Phantom,    | PRIMARY    |
  |           |                   | slots since    | Backpack,   |            |
  |           |                   | 25 Aug 2026)   | exchanges   |            |
  | Base      | ~$0.01            | ~2s soft,      | Coinbase    | PRIMARY    |
  |           |                   | ~15 min hard   | Wallet,     |            |
  |           |                   |                | MetaMask    |            |
  | Polygon   | ~$0.01            | seconds        | MetaMask    | ALLOWED    |

 09. DECISION 4: BUILD OUR OWN WALLET, OR USE A PAYMENT GATEWAY?
================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |
  | We could publish our own wallet address and ask people to send money    |
  | there. That is like running a shop where customers throw cash through a  |
  | window with no receipt. A gateway gives every payment an invoice,       |
  | checks it, and deposits normal money in the bank. 70% of merchants use   |
  | one. We will too.                                                        |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+

  9.1  SELF-CUSTODY vs GATEWAY

  +----------------------------+--------------------------+-------------------+
  |                            | Self-custody (own wallet)| Gateway           |
  +----------------------------+--------------------------+-------------------+
  | Keys / passwords           | We guard them. Lose =    | Gateway guards    |
  |                            | money gone forever       | them              |
  | Match payment to customer  | We build it              | Built in          |
  | Wrong amount / late / dup  | Support tickets          | Rules built in    |
  | Sanctions screening        | Our legal problem        | Built in          |
  | Convert to dollars         | Manual via exchange      | Automatic         |
  | Accounting export          | We build it              | Built in          |
  | Regulatory exposure        | We may look like a       | Gateway is the    |
  |                            | custodian / transmitter  | licensed party    |
  | Cost                       | Gas only + eng time +    | 1-1.5% per payment|
  |                            | risk                     |                   |
  +----------------------------+--------------------------+-------------------+

    Market evidence: 70.1% of documented stablecoin-accepting merchants use
    a third-party gateway; almost none take direct wallet transfers.

  9.2  GATEWAY SHORTLIST

  +----------------------+-----------+------------------------------------------+
  | Provider             | Fee       | Notes                                    |
  +----------------------+-----------+------------------------------------------+
  | 1. Stripe stablecoin | 1.5%      | Same dashboard/API as cards. Settles to  |
  |    payments          |           | Stripe balance in local currency. Works  |
  |                      |           | in Checkout, Elements, Billing,          |
  |                      |           | Invoicing, Payment Links. Recurring:     |
  |                      |           | supported. Coins: USDC on Solana, Base,  |
  |                      |           | Polygon, Ethereum, Tempo (+USDP/USDG     |
  |                      |           | US-only). LIMITS: US-based businesses    |
  |                      |           | only; $10,000 per payment; refunds only  |
  |                      |           | to the sending wallet; priced in USD.    |
  |                      |           | Note: Link stablecoins is a separate     |
  |                      |           | product (0.8% promo to 1 Jan 2027).      |
  | 2. Coinbase Commerce | 1.0%      | Standalone hosted checkout. Base,        |
  |                      |           | Ethereum, Polygon, Solana. USD payout    |
  |                      |           | via add-on.                              |
  | 3. CoinGate          | varies    | MiCA-licensed (Bank of Lithuania). Good  |
  |                      |           | if billing entity is in the EU.          |
  |                      |           | Supports fiat settlement + API payouts.  |
  | 4. Crypto-native MoR | varies    | e.g. Fungies. Handles tax AND crypto.    |
  |    (e.g. Fungies)    |           | Smaller, less proven.                    |
  | X. Paddle            | n/a       | NO native stablecoin checkout. Can't do  |
  |                      |           | this on Paddle alone.                    |
  +----------------------+-----------+------------------------------------------+

  9.3  THE PADDLE PROBLEM

    The Money Model says "Paddle now, Stripe later". Paddle has no crypto
    checkout. So stablecoins force one of three paths:

      PATH 1: Move billing to Stripe NOW (cards + USDC in one place).
              + one system, native meters/credits (Money Model page 4)
              - we take on global tax compliance (use Stripe Tax)
              - needs a US entity for stablecoins

      PATH 2: Keep Paddle for cards + add Coinbase Commerce for USDC.
              + no migration
              - two systems to reconcile; we own tax on USDC sales

      PATH 3: Keep Paddle, delay stablecoins.
              + zero work
              - no margin gain, no access gain

    RECOMMENDATION: PATH 1 if a US entity exists or is planned.
                    PATH 2 as a temporary bridge if not.

  9.4  CHECKLIST FOR CHOOSING A GATEWAY (ask every vendor)

    [ ] All-in cost on a $1,000 payment, with FX spread in basis points
    [ ] Underpayment tolerance: configurable? who covers the shortfall?
    [ ] Wrong-network recovery: possible? fee? how long?
    [ ] Late payment after quote expiry: what happens?
    [ ] Rolling reserve: any %? for how long? (some hold 5-10% for 30-180d)
    [ ] Payout schedule and fees to our bank
    [ ] Refund method (to wallet? partial?)
    [ ] Recurring / subscription support
    [ ] AML / sanctions screening included?
    [ ] Countries where OUR entity is allowed
    [ ] Accounting export format (per-payment tx hash, rate, fee)
    [ ] Webhook reliability, retries, idempotency
    [ ] Uptime history / status page
    [ ] Does the vendor change supported coins without notice?

 11. FIAT ABSTRACTION: USERS SEE Rs / $, NEVER "CRYPTO"
================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |
  | The customer should think in rupees or dollars. "USDC" is just a        |
  | button, like "UPI" or "Card". Behind the scenes, we work out how many    |
  | USDC equals the price, lock it for a few minutes, and handle all the    |
  | conversion and bookkeeping. The customer never sees exchange rates,      |
  | blockchains, or crypto jargon unless they want to.                       |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+

  11.1  WHAT THE USER SEES

      +--------------------------------------------------+
      |  Prime                                           |
      |  Rs 2,999 / month      (or $39 / EUR 39)         |
      |  5,000 credits every month                       |
      |                                                  |
      |  Pay with:                                       |
      |   [ Card ]   [ UPI ]   [ USDC  +5% credits ]     |
      +--------------------------------------------------+

  11.2  WHAT THE SYSTEM DOES UNDERNEATH

    STEP 1 - PRICE      Look up fixed local price point from the quarterly
                        price table (Money Model: $39 / EUR 39 / GBP 33 /
                        Rs 2,999). Never live FX.
    STEP 2 - QUOTE      Convert to a USD amount for the USDC path
                        (e.g. Rs 2,999 -> $36.00 from our price table).
    STEP 3 - LOCK       Lock that USDC amount for 30 minutes.
    STEP 4 - PRESENT    Show exact USDC amount + network + estimated gas.
    STEP 5 - DETECT     Gateway watches chain, matches to invoice.
    STEP 6 - SETTLE     Gateway converts USDC -> USD; webhook "paid".
    STEP 7 - GRANT      Entitlement service grants credits.
    STEP 8 - RECORD     Ledger writes one immutable row (Section 14).

  11.3  RULES

    R1. Credits are the ONLY unit inside HERE. Card, UPI and USDC buyers get
        the same credits for the same plan.
    R2. The ledger never holds currency or crypto. (Money Model rule.)
    R3. Never display a live exchange rate like "36.42 USDC". Round quotes to
        whole cents of our price table.
    R4. Gas is shown before signing. No surprise costs.
    R5. The word "crypto" appears only on the USDC payment screen and its
        help page. Nowhere else in the product.
    R6. Stripe's stablecoin checkout presents in USD (other currencies in
        private preview). Local-currency display is OUR job via step 2.

                        T+3 retry

      Refund requested
           |
           v
      Why? -------- job failed on our side --> credits restored automatically

 15. UNIT ECONOMICS
================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |
  | Every payment method charges us a fee. USDC's fee is much smaller. We   |
  | give part of that saving back to the customer as bonus credits (so they  |
  | have a reason to choose it) and keep the rest as extra profit. The rule: |
  | the bonus must always cost us LESS than the fee we save.                |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+

  15.1  FEE PER PAYMENT

  +-------------+----------------------+----------------------+-------------------+
  | Payment     | Paddle (5% + $0.50)  | Stripe card          | Stripe USDC (1.5%)|
  |             |                      | (2.9% + $0.30)       |                   |
  +-------------+----------------------+----------------------+-------------------+
  | $10 top-up  | $1.00   (10.0%)      | $0.59   (5.9%)       | $0.15   (1.5%)    |
  | $25 top-up  | $1.75   ( 7.0%)      | $1.03   (4.1%)       | $0.38   (1.5%)    |
  | Prime $39   | $2.45   ( 6.3%)      | $1.43   (3.7%)       | $0.59   (1.5%)    |
  | Studio $199 | $10.45  ( 5.3%)      | $6.07   (3.1%)       | $2.99   (1.5%)    |
  | World $999  | $50.45  ( 5.1%)      | $29.27  (2.9%)       | $14.99  (1.5%)    |
  +-------------+----------------------+----------------------+-------------------+
   Card figures are US domestic; international cards cost more, which makes
   USDC relatively better for non-US buyers. Gas (~$0.01) is paid by the
   customer, not us.

      Fee on a Prime $39 payment
      Paddle |#########################################| $2.45
      Card   |########################                 | $1.43
      USDC   |##########                               | $0.59

  15.2  WHAT A BONUS CREDIT COSTS US

    Credits are sold at $0.01 list value, but cost us ~$0.002 each to serve
    (Money Model target, 80% margin). So:

      Bonus of 250 credits  = $2.50 of LIST value to the customer
                            = up to $0.50 of REAL cost to us
                              (only if every bonus credit is actually used)

    This is why bonus credits beat price discounts: a $2.50 discount costs
    us $2.50; 250 bonus credits cost us at most $0.50.

  15.3  THE BONUS SCHEDULE (tiered so it never loses money)

    Rule: bonus cost must be LESS than the fee saving versus a normal
    card payment (the worst case, since card is cheaper than Paddle).

  +-------------+--------+---------+----------+-----------+-----------+---------+
  | Plan        | Bonus  | Bonus   | Max cost | Saving vs | Saving vs | Net vs  |
  |             | %      | credits | to us    | card      | Paddle    | card    |
  +-------------+--------+---------+----------+-----------+-----------+---------+
  | $10 top-up  | 5%     |    50   |  $0.10   |  $0.44    |  $0.85    | +$0.34  |
  | $25 top-up  | 5%     |   125   |  $0.25   |  $0.65    |  $1.38    | +$0.40  |
  | Prime $39   | 5%     |   250   |  $0.50   |  $0.85    |  $1.86    | +$0.35  |
  | Studio $199 | 3%     |   900   |  $1.80   |  $3.09    |  $7.47    | +$1.29  |
  | World $999  | 2%     | 3,500   |  $7.00   | $14.29    | $35.47    | +$7.29  |
  +-------------+--------+---------+----------+-----------+-----------+---------+
   Bonus credits computed on each plan's monthly credits
   (Prime 5,000 / Studio 30,000 / World 175,000) and on top-up credits
   (100 per $1). "Net vs Paddle" is much larger: e.g. Prime +$1.36.

    WARNING - why not 5% everywhere?
      World at 5% = 8,750 bonus credits = up to $17.50 cost,
      but saving vs card is only $14.29 -> we would LOSE $3.21 per payment.
      Tiering fixes this.

  15.4  CONTRIBUTION PER PRIME ACCOUNT (monthly, before guardian cost)

      Money Model: Prime contributes ~$29.97/month before guardian cost.

                          Paddle      Card        USDC (+5% bonus)
      Price               $39.00      $39.00      $39.00
      Processing fee     -$2.45      -$1.43      -$0.59
      Bonus credit cost    -           -          -$0.50 (max)
      -----------------------------------------------------------
      Difference vs                  +$1.02      +$1.36 vs Paddle
      Paddle baseline                             +$0.35 vs card

  15.5  WHAT THIS IS WORTH AT SCALE (illustrative)

    Using the Money Model's month-12 Plan B: 1,120 paying accounts.
    Assume USDC share of payments = 5%.

      56 accounts x ~$1.36 saving vs Paddle ...... ~$76 / month
      + top-ups and larger plans ................. more

    Honest reading: at 5% adoption this is small money. The fee saving
    matters more if (a) USDC share grows, (b) we move ALL billing to Stripe
    anyway (card saving vs Paddle on every payment is much bigger), or
    (c) it unlocks customers who otherwise could not pay at all - that
    revenue is the real prize, and it is 100% incremental.

  15.6  NEW THING UNLOCKED: SMALL TOP-UPS

    With no fixed fee, we can safely allow smaller top-ups for USDC payers:

      $2 top-up  : Card fee $0.36 (18%)  vs  USDC fee $0.03 (1.5%)
      $5 top-up  : Card fee $0.45 ( 9%)  vs  USDC fee $0.08 (1.5%)

    Proposal: USDC minimum top-up = $2 (card minimum stays $10).

  15.7  HIDDEN COSTS TO MODEL

    [ ] FX spread when USD is paid out to an INR or EUR bank account.
    [ ] Tax compliance: Paddle's 5% includes global VAT/GST as MoR.
        Stripe's 1.5% does not. Add Stripe Tax fees + filing cost.
    [ ] Rolling reserves some gateways hold (5-10%, 30-180 days).
    [ ] Support cost per USDC ticket (expect higher at first).
    [ ] India entity: 1% TDS + 30% VDA tax on any gain [TAX].
    [ ] Engineering: ~2-4 weeks for Phase 3-4 if on Stripe already.

================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |
  | Crypto payments fail in different ways than cards. Most failures are     |

  | each one, we decide the rule in advance so no customer is ever confused  |
  | and no support person has to guess.                                     |

  16.1  PAYMENT STATE MACHINE

      CREATED --> AWAITING_PAYMENT --(tx seen)--> CONFIRMING
                        |                              |
                   (30 min, no tx)          +----------+----------+

                        v                  PAID    UNDERPAID  OVERPAID
                     EXPIRED                |          |          |
                        |                   v          v          v
                 (tx arrives late)      credits    tolerance?  credit
                        |               granted    yes -> PAID  excess as
                        v                          no  -> PARTIAL bonus
                  LATE_PAYMENT                         (credit what
                  (accept within 24h                    arrived)
                   at original quote)

  16.2  POLICY TABLE

  +----+-------------------+------------------------------+---------------------------+
  | #  | Failure           | What happens                 | HERE policy               |
  +----+-------------------+------------------------------+---------------------------+
  | F1 | Underpayment      | Customer paid a bit less     | Auto-accept within 1% or  |

  |    |                   |                              | ask for the difference.   |

  16.3  CHARGEBACKS: CARD vs USDC IN ONE PICTURE

      CARD:  Customer --(complains to bank)--> Bank pulls money back
             Merchant loses money + dispute fee, must send evidence.

      USDC:  Customer --(complains to US)--> We decide using our policy.
             No one can pull money back without us.
             Risk shifts from "fraud reversal" to "our refund policy".

 17. TREASURY POLICY (WHAT WE DO WITH THE MONEY)
================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |
  | Treasury = how a company manages its cash. Our policy is simple: turn   |
  | every digital dollar into a real dollar immediately. The only exception  |
  | is a small, capped pot of USDC kept for paying people abroad in USDC.    |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+

  T1. DEFAULT: 100% auto-conversion to USD at the gateway.
  T2. EXPOSURE: seconds-to-minutes between payment and conversion ~ zero.
  T3. FLOAT (only once USDC payouts exist):
        - Size   : max 30 days of expected USDC payouts
        - Asset  : USDC only
        - Where  : regulated custodian (Stripe stablecoin financial account
                   or Coinbase). NEVER a self-custody wallet.
        - Review : monthly
  T4. NO YIELD CHASING: no lending, staking or "earn" products. (Regulated
      stablecoins can't pay holders interest under GENIUS anyway.)
  T5. KILL SWITCH: if USDC trades below $0.98 for >1 hour, pause USDC
      checkout and payouts until it recovers.
  T6. REPORTING: monthly report of USDC received, converted, fees, float
      balance, incidents.

      Money flow with float (later phase)

      Customers pay USDC --> Gateway --> 100% converted --> USD bank
                                                              |
                                          (only for payouts)  |
                                                              v
      Guardians/creators <-- USDC float (<= 30 days) <-- USD topped up

 18. TAX, LEGAL AND COMPLIANCE
================================================================================

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  | IN PLAIN WORDS                                                           |
  | Accepting digital dollars is legal in most places, but each country has  |
  | its own rules. The two big risks: (1) accidentally becoming a "money    |
  | business" (which needs licences), and (2) tax treatment in India. A     |
  | one-hour lawyer and accountant review BEFORE launch avoids both.        |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------------+

  18.1  CREDITS ARE STILL NOT MONEY (Money Model rule, reinforced)

    ALLOWED  : Customer pays IN with USDC -> receives credits.
    FORBIDDEN: Cashing credits OUT as USDC.
    FORBIDDEN: Sending credits between users for value.
    FORBIDDEN: Promising credits can be redeemed for USDC.
    Each forbidden item edges us toward money-transmitter regulation.

      IN  (safe)                     OUT (forbidden)
      USDC --> credits               credits --X--> USDC

  18.2  UNITED STATES
    - GENIUS Act (July 2025) regulates stablecoin ISSUERS, not merchants.
      Effective 18 Jan 2027 or 120 days after final rules, whichever is
      earlier. Federal Reserve published its proposed rule 29 Sep 2026.
    - Merchant duties: normal sales tax (Stripe Tax), normal income tax.
      Stripe handles wallet screening.
    - [LEGAL] Confirm credit terms + USDC acceptance with a US lawyer.

  18.3  EUROPEAN UNION
    - MiCA in force. Use only MiCA-compliant coins (USDC, EURC).
    - VAT on digital services still applies by customer country.
    - Leo is in Prague; if an EU entity bills, use a MiCA-licensed gateway.

  18.4  INDIA  [TAX] [LEGAL]
    - Stablecoins are VDAs (Section 2(47A)).
    - 30% flat tax on gains (Section 115BBH), 1% TDS on transfers (194S).
    - Converting USDC to INR is a taxable transfer.
    - VDA service providers must be FIU-IND registered.
    - Crypto brought into CARF/FATCA tax reporting from 2026.
    - Compliant route for an Indian business: regulated provider accepts
      and converts OFFSHORE, remits INR with eFIRA + purpose code matched
      to export invoice.
    - Indian CUSTOMERS paying us: they are transferring a VDA -> their own
      TDS/tax situation. Recommend UPI as the default for Indian users.

  18.5  COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST BEFORE LAUNCH
    [ ] Lawyer review of credit terms + USDC acceptance (billing country)
    [ ] CA review if any Indian entity touches crypto
    [ ] Gateway contract: AML/sanctions screening confirmed
    [ ] Terms of service updated: USDC refunds only to sending wallet
    [ ] Privacy policy: wallet addresses are personal data
    [ ] Tax engine configured for USDC sales (Stripe Tax)
    [ ] Accounting: USDC receipts booked as deferred revenue

 24. APPENDIX

  24.1  WORKED EXAMPLE 1 - A creator in Manila tops up $25

    1. Sees "Add credits - $25". Chooses USDC. Quote: 25.00 USDC ->
       2,500 credits + 125 bonus = 2,625 credits.
    2. Connects Phantom, picks Solana, signs. Gas: ~$0.001.
    3. Confirmed in ~1 second. Stripe converts to USD.
    4. HERE receives $25.00 - $0.375 fee = $24.625.
    5. Credits: 2,500 to Purchased (12 months), 125 to Promo (30 days).
    6. Cost of bonus to us: 125 x $0.002 = $0.25 max.
    7. Versus card: fee would have been $1.03. We saved $0.65, gave
       back $0.25, kept +$0.40.

  24.2  WORKED EXAMPLE 2 - An underpayment

    Invoice: 39.00 USDC. Customer withdraws from an exchange that deducts
    a $0.30 fee. 38.70 USDC arrives.
    Shortfall $0.30 < tolerance ($0.50) -> treated as PAID in full.
    Cost to us: $0.30. Cost of a support ticket: much more. Correct call.

  24.3  WORKED EXAMPLE 3 - Why the World bonus is 2%, not 5%

    World: $999, 175,000 credits/month.
    Card fee $29.27, USDC fee $14.99 -> saving $14.29.
    5% bonus = 8,750 credits x $0.002 = $17.50  -> LOSS of $3.21.
    2% bonus = 3,500 credits x $0.002 = $7.00   -> GAIN of $7.29.

  24.4  WORKED EXAMPLE 4 - Indian user, Indian price

    Sees Rs 2,999. Options: UPI (recommended), Card, USDC.
    If USDC: system quotes $36.00 from the price table -> 36.00 USDC.
    Note: the Indian customer is transferring a VDA and may face their own
    1% TDS / tax treatment. That is why UPI is the recommended default for
    Indian users.
