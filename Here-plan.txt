PART 0 — THE ORIGINAL ASK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The user asked, as a product manager: what is this kind of document called;
what should we measure (the North Star); what structure of things do we need
to be maximally profitable; how do we manage the $500/month cost of a
Guardian; how do we build a credit logic for every action; two plans with
flowcharts for both credit and billing; think like a monetization PM.

His own framing, verbatim and central to every decision below:
  "Credits = user trust, control, and perceived value."
  "Billing = unit economics, revenue, and profitability."
These are treated as two separate systems with two separate jobs throughout.


PART 1 — THE CORE DECISION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[v2] There are now TWO fixed costs that do not scale with usage: the
$500/month Guardian, and the Google Cloud bill for keeping users' published
surfaces alive (Part 6a / Part 10b). Both run whether anyone is active or not,
so the conclusion below is unchanged — it just has a higher floor to clear.

There is one fixed cost that does not scale with usage: the $500/month
Guardian. A pure pay-as-you-go credit model cannot carry a fixed cost — it
only earns when someone is active, but the cost runs whether they are active
or not.

DECISION: a TWO-PART TARIFF.
  - A subscription floor pays for fixed costs (guardians, storage, support,
    always-on infrastructure).
  - Metered credits pay for variable costs (model tokens, renders,
    transcription, egress), priced with margin on top.
  - Credits never carry the fixed cost. The subscription never carries the
    variable cost. Each part covers what it is shaped like.

The credit system's job: make people feel in control. Predictable prices, a
visible balance, nothing charged for failure, "this will cost X" shown before
anything runs. If someone is ever surprised by a charge, the credit system
has failed — even if the accounting was correct.

The billing system's job: make the company survive. Recurring revenue, gross
margin, cash collection, deferred-revenue accounting, dunning, tax. Should be
nearly invisible to the user — one clean invoice.

THE TRAP TO AVOID: never let the credit PRICE float with the underlying COST
("this run cost us more, so it costs you more"). It destroys trust and makes
the product impossible to budget for. Fix the price users see; manage the
cost internally (model routing, caching, or eating it).


PART 2 — THE ANCHOR AND THE MARGIN TARGET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1 credit = $0.01 of list value. 100 credits = $1.
  Target internal cost: $0.002 per credit.
  Every price on the rate card = 5× our cost = 80% gross margin.
  If a new action can't be priced at 5× its cost, it does not ship until it
  can.

  THE ONE EXCEPTION (added v2) — INFRASTRUCTURE IS PRICED AT 3×, NOT 5×:

    Action class                          | Markup | Target GM | Why
    --------------------------------------|--------|-----------|------------------
    Model & media actions                 | 5×     | 80%       | nobody can price-
      (room turns, guardian runs,         |        |           | check a Sonnet run
       transcription, render, video)      |        |           |
    Infrastructure                        | 3×     | 65-70%    | Cloud Run, Replit
      (hosting, compute, requests,        |        |           | and Emergent all
       egress, datastores) — Tier 8       |        |           | publish unit rates;
                                          |        |           | 5× is checkable and
                                          |        |           | it gets checked
    Third-party pass-through (Tier 7)     | ×1.35  | 23-26%    | unchanged
                                          | ×1.30  |           |

  Market evidence for the 3×: Replit resells a ~$5 machine as a $15/mo Reserved
  VM; Emergent charges 50 credits (~$10/mo) per deployed app on infra that
  costs it a few dollars. Both land near 3×. Nobody in this category holds 5×
  on commodity compute, because the buyer can read Google's price list.

  HARD FLOOR is unchanged and applies to both classes: never sell a credit
  below $0.004.
  HARD FLOOR: never sell a credit below $0.004 (2× cost, 50% margin). This is
  the lowest an enterprise negotiation may reach — write it down before the
  first big customer asks for a discount, because in the room you'll want to
  say yes anyway.


PART 3 — NORTH STAR AND METRIC TREE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NORTH STAR: Weekly Successful Credit Burn (WSCB) — credits settled on jobs
that COMPLETED and were KEPT (not discarded, not refunded), summed every
week.

Why this number: it only rises when three things are true at once — people
are active, they're doing work valuable enough to keep, and the company is
getting paid. It cannot be gamed by breaking things (failed jobs are free by
policy, see Part 5). It leads revenue by 2-4 weeks, so it doubles as an early
warning.

SIX BRANCHES (each with one number to move weekly):

  1. ACQUISITION
     Signups, cost to acquire, share reaching a first GO within 24 hours.
     Target: 60% of signups reach a first GO on day one.

  2. ACTIVATION
     Time to First GO — minutes from sign-in to the first job that finishes.
     Single best predictor of whether someone ever pays.
     Target: median under 8 minutes.

  3. ENGAGEMENT
     Weekly active Prime members, live Scircles, GOs per active per week.
     Target: 12 GOs per active per week. Below 4/week = visiting, not playing.

  4. CONSUMPTION
     Burn depth — % of the monthly allotment actually used.
     Healthy band: 60-80%. Under 30% predicts churn. Over 95% means rationing
     (people stop exploring, which is where expansion revenue comes from).

  5. ECONOMICS
     Cost per credit, margin per credit, accounts per guardian.
     Target: $0.002 cost/credit, 80% margin, 80+ accounts per guardian.

  6. RETENTION
     Net revenue retention (NRR), logo churn, refund/waste rate.
     Target: NRR above 110%, waste below 2% of credits burned.

GUARDRAILS (never trade away, even while pushing the North Star up):
  - Credits charged for jobs that failed on our side:      0%, no exceptions
  - "I didn't expect that charge" reports:                 < 1 per 1,000 GOs
  - Accounts burning over 95% of their allotment:           < 15%
  - Breakage (credits sold, expired unused):                < 8% of sales
  - Accounts per guardian:                                  ≥ 50

Review cadence: one meeting a week, one screen — this week's burn vs last
week's, then the six branch numbers, then the five guardrails. Burn up and
nothing red = ship more of whatever you just shipped. Burn up and something
red = you did not actually win, you borrowed from next quarter.


PART 4 — THE MODEL-COST PROBLEM AND THE MULTIPLIER FIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Guardians offer four Claude models whose cost differs by 10× for the
identical action (see HERE-PROJECT-CONTEXT.txt §5 for the price table). A
flat credit price per action either overcharges Haiku users or loses money
on every Fable run. Fix: give each model a multiplier tuned so margin stays
at 80% regardless of which model is picked, computed from real per-run cost
at a 20K-input / 2K-output run (Guardians' own stated typical run size):

  Tier       | Model       | Cost (20K in/2K out) | Credits | Price | Margin | Multiplier
  -----------|-------------|-----------------------|---------|-------|--------|------------
  Swift      | Haiku 4.5   | $0.030                | 15      | $0.15 | 80%    | ×0.5
  Standard   | Sonnet 5    | $0.060                | 30      | $0.30 | 80%    | ×1   (default)
  Deep       | Opus 5      | $0.150                | 75      | $0.75 | 80%    | ×2.5
  Cinematic  | Fable 5.1   | $0.300                | 150     | $1.50 | 80%    | ×5

  (Formula: credits = round(cost_usd × 5 / 0.01). Verified by direct
  computation, not eyeballed — all four land at exactly 80% margin.)

TWO CHANGES THIS IMPLIES FOR THE PRODUCT:
  (a) Guardians' current DEFAULT model is Opus 5 — a ×2.5 multiplier on the
      most common action in the product. Recommendation: make Sonnet 5
      ("Standard") the default and let Deep be a deliberate choice on the GO
      card. Estimated worth ~$5,700/month at month-12 Plan-B scale (see
      Part 10's margin-lever table), and it's a one-line config change.
  (b) Turn on prompt caching for Scircle/room context — the most repeated
      payload in the system, at roughly a tenth the price when cached.


PART 5 — THE SEVEN CREDIT RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Price the verb, not the token. People can't reason about tokens,
     seconds or gigabytes — the rate card lists things a person wanted to
     happen ("transcribe this recording"), never raw compute units.
  2. Reading is free. Doing costs. Browsing, replaying a recording you
     already made, joining a Scircle, opening NOW — always $0. Charge only
     for machine work we had to buy.
  3. Failure is free. If a job fails on our side, the hold is VOIDED —
     never charged in the first place, not "refunded afterward." This one
     rule buys more goodwill than any amount of free credits.
  4. Nothing runs without a GO. Opening a recording, reading a message,
     selecting a card, or joining a group must never silently start a paid
     job.
  5. Quote before, settle after. Show an estimate with a band, reserve
     (hold) it, then charge the MEASURED amount (always ≤ the quote). If it
     came in cheaper, the user keeps the difference — people remember that.
  6. One currency inside, many outside. The ledger only ever counts
     credits. Currency conversion happens at the "shop window" (the buy
     screen), never inside the meter — this keeps FX entirely out of unit
     economics.
  7. The price is fixed. The cost is ours to manage. A credit price never
     moves because our COGS moved. If a model gets more expensive, route to
     a cheaper one, cache harder, or eat it — the user's budget is a
     promise. This holds for hosting too: if Google raises egress, we cache
     harder or absorb it.

  --- ADDED IN V2, FOR STANDING (RECURRING) CHARGES ---

  8. Publishing is free. Staying live is not. The publish button itself
     states the monthly figure BEFORE the first deploy. This is the exact
     place Emergent lost user trust: a 50-credit/month (~$10) per-app
     deployment fee that is not shown at plan-selection time and quietly
     consumes half a Standard plan.
  9. A standing meter gets a RENEWAL GO, not a one-time GO. Monthly cost
     preview, budget cap and stop condition, every cycle — not once at
     creation. Part 12 already requires this of the STANDING tool class;
     hosting is the first real instance of it.
 10. Sleep, never delete. Out of credits → the surface SLEEPS behind a
     "wake this up" page. Data retained 60 days, mirroring the dunning
     ladder in Part 11. We never delete someone's live site to collect a
     debt.
 11. Scale to zero by default. Always-on is a deliberate upgrade the user
     chooses, the same interaction as picking Deep on the model picker.
 12. Publish the unit rates. Replit publishes its per-compute-unit and
     per-request rates and is trusted for it. Emergent hides its deployment
     fee and takes the reputational hit. Our whole credit doctrine says show
     the price first — that must include infrastructure.

  every workspace — free tier included — 20 Cloud credits and 4 AI credits a
  month so that publishing never eats the build budget. Without it, people
  build something, discover it costs money to show anyone, and never share
  it. That kills the only organic growth loop a build product has.

  *** THE ENVELOPE IS A REAL COGS LINE, NOT A FREE LUNCH ***
  Google's free tier — 180,000 vCPU-seconds, 360,000 GiB-seconds, 2M requests,
  one free Firestore database — is PER BILLING ACCOUNT, NOT PER APP. It is
  consumed once across every customer app we host, not once each. Every
  "hosting is basically free for small apps" intuition from the consumer
  tools fails here, because in those tools somebody else is absorbing it. On
  our own GCP account we are that somebody. Model the envelope as a capped
  marketing expense in COGS (Part 9 does).

THE THREE OPERATIONAL FACTS THAT SET THESE PRICES
  1. Free tier is per billing account (above). The envelope costs us money.
  2. Egress is $0.12/GB, not $0.085 (the Tier 6 correction). Cloud CDN in
     front of every static surface is therefore mandatory.
  3. Custom domains may require a Global External Application Load Balancer
     forwarding rule at ~$18-25/month EACH. At that cost the $1.50 SKU is
     mispriced by roughly 16×. It only works at $1.50 if many customer
     domains host-route through ONE shared LB with SNI. This is an
     architecture decision that sets a price — see Part 14 Q8. BLOCKING.

TWO PRODUCT DEFAULTS THAT ARE REALLY PRICING DECISIONS
  (a) New Guardian-built apps default to FIRESTORE, not Cloud SQL. Cloud SQL's
      smallest Postgres has a hard $8-10/month floor per database and no SLA;
      a $30/month Postgres SKU on a $39 Prime plan nearly doubles that
      customer's bill. Firestore scales from zero. Make managed Postgres a
      Studio-and-above choice. The cheapest way to control a cost line is to
      not create it.
  (b) Scale-to-zero is the default; always-on is the $30 upgrade.

  Confirmed by the product owner (21 Sep): Guardians build everything today,

  RECOMMENDATION: declare the meter now and ship it DISABLED. Add a

HOW TIER 8 INTERACTS WITH THE WALLET (amends Part 7)
  Hosting draws from PLAN credits first, never from PROMO. A 7-day welcome
  grant must never silently buy a month of hosting the user did not know they
  had started — they would discover it only when the grant expired and their
  site went quiet. This is a deliberate exception to the soonest-expiry-first
  spend order, and it is the only one.

PART 7 — THE WALLET: FOUR BUCKETS, ONE SPEND ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A wallet is not one number — it's four buckets that always spend in the same
order (soonest-to-expire first). Show this order in NOW; it's the thing
people most often accuse credit products of cheating on.

  Order | Bucket      | Source                              | Expires
  ------|-------------|--------------------------------------|------------------
  1st   | Promo       | Onboarding grant, campaign, apology  | 7-30 days
  2nd   | Plan        | This month's subscription allotment  | End of cycle,
        |             |                                        +30 days grace
  3rd   | Purchased   | Top-ups paid in cash                 | 12 months
  4th   | Overdraft   | Studio and World tiers only           | Settles on next
        |             |                                        invoice

Plan credits deliberately do NOT roll over indefinitely (only one cycle of
30-day grace) — rollover feels generous but quietly converts a subscription
into a credit bank, encourages stockpiling, and grows unburned liability
without matching revenue.


PART 8 — THE CREDIT FLOW (the "GO gate")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every metered action walks this exact path, every time:

  1. QUOTE   — units × rate, computed from the tool's manifest (see Part 12),
               with a ± band.
  2. GO CARD — the trust moment. Shows what runs, which model, the estimated
               credits, a hard cap, and one tap to change any of it. NOTHING
               RUNS BEFORE THIS.
  3. Balance check: is balance ≥ estimate × 1.2 (a 20% buffer)?
       - If NO: offer top-up / switch to a cheaper model / trim scope /
         cancel. Never silently charge. Auto-recharge only fires if the user
         explicitly opted into it beforehand.
       - If YES: continue.
  4. HOLD    — reserve the estimate. This is NOT a charge yet.
  5. RUN     — meter the real units as the job actually executes.
  6. Outcome:
       - COMPLETED — settle the MEASURED amount, release the rest of the
         hold. User keeps the difference between quote and actual.
       - PARTIAL   — charge only the units actually measured, flag the job
         for review.
       - FAILED (our side) — void the hold entirely. Zero credits charged.
         The retry is free.
  7. LEDGER  — one immutable row: person, tool@version, model, units,
               credits, job id, Scircle, who authorised it, what it actually
               cost us.
  8. A receipt appears in NOW; the wallet updates; the history is
     exportable.
  9. Every ledger row feeds back into the tool template — this is the
     mechanism for the RSI (recursive self-improvement) loop the user asked
     for (see Part 12): the "credits per successful outcome" per tool
     version becomes an optimisation target.

The estimate×1.2 hold buffer specifically prevents the worst support ticket
in any credit product — a job that dies halfway because the balance ran out
mid-run.

PART 10 — FIXED COST ONE: THE $500 GUARDIAN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[v2] There are now two fixed costs. This part covers the guardian; Part 10b
covers the live-surfaces fleet. They have different shapes and need different
operating rules: a guardian is a STEP cost bought in $500 blocks and managed
by utilisation; hosting is a CREEPING cost that arrives one published app at
a time and is managed by defaults and sleep policy.

This is the single most important derived number in the whole plan.

A Prime account ($39/mo) contributes, BEFORE the guardian's fixed cost:
  $39.00 (price)
  - $6.50  (3,250 of its 5,000 credits burned, at $0.002/credit cost)
  - $1.43  (payment processing, 2.9% + $0.30)
  - $1.10  (storage/base infra)
  = $29.97 contribution margin per account, before guardian cost.

BREAK-EVEN accounts-per-guardian (APG) = 500 / 29.97 = 16.68 → 17 accounts.
  (Formula: cm(APG) = (29.97 - 500/APG) / 39. Solved directly, not
  eyeballed — verified: APG=20→12.7%, APG=50→51.2%, APG=80→60.8% margin.)

  APG      | Contribution margin %
  ---------|------------------------
  17       | 0% (break-even)
  50       | 51% (alert floor)
  80       | 61% (TARGET — and the curve flattens sharply above this, so
           |      pushing to 140 buys almost nothing more)

A dedicated guardian is NOT a feature bundled into a plan — it is a SKU,
sold separately at $899/month:
  Tier                        | Price | Credits burned | Contribution before | Break-even
                              |       |                 | guardian             | APG
  -----------------------------|-------|-----------------|----------------------|------------
  Prime                        | $39   | 3,250 ($6.50)   | $29.97               | 17
  Studio                       | $199  | 19,500 ($39.00) | $149.93              | 3.4
  World                        | $999  | 105,000 ($210)  | $744.73              | 0.7
  Dedicated Guardian (add-on)  | $899  | billed separate | $399 (44% margin)    | n/a

  DO NOT bundle a dedicated guardian into the $999 World plan — that single
  line would consume half the plan price and drag it to ~25% margin,
  turning best customers into worst customers. Sell it separately.
  Absolute floor on the add-on price: $833/mo (40% margin). Never go below
  $715/mo (30%) even for an anchor customer.

FOUR OPERATING RULES FOR GUARDIAN CAPACITY:
  1. Never provision a guardian on hope. Only add one when there's
     $1,600/month of COMMITTED, attributed revenue behind it (a 2.5×
     coverage ratio at ~78% margin). Queue depth is not a reason; revenue is.
  2. Measure Guardian Coverage Ratio (GCR) monthly = gross profit
     attributed to that guardian ÷ $500.
       Target ≥ 2.5×.  Warn at < 1.5×.  Act (consolidate & retire) at
       < 1.0× for two consecutive months.
  3. Hibernate idle dedicated guardians: under 10% utilisation for 14 days
     → sleep it, prorate the customer's next invoice. Stop paying for air.
  4. Rebalance shared pools weekly (not reactively on incident) to keep
     every pool inside 60-120 accounts. Below 60 = burning margin. Above
     120 = buying queue latency that shows up as churn.

THIS WHOLE SECTION RESTS ON ONE UNVERIFIED ASSUMPTION: that one guardian can
serve many accounts concurrently (ceiling assumed ~120). If a "guardian" is
actually one-to-one with a customer, or is a human operator with a real hour
budget, APG collapses to 1 and EVERY PRICE IN THIS DOCUMENT roughly triples.
This is the single most important open question — see Part 14, Q1.


PART 10b — FIXED COST TWO: THE LIVE-SURFACES FLEET (added v2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A guardian is a step cost: $500 at a time, managed by accounts-per-guardian.
Hosting is the opposite shape — small, continuous, and it only ever grows,
one published app at a time. That is exactly why it is easy to leave out of a
pricing model and expensive to leave out of a business.

THE THREE GOOGLE CLOUD FACTS THAT DRIVE THIS SECTION are stated in full in
Part 6a: the free tier is per billing account (so the envelope is real COGS),
egress is $0.12/GB (so Cloud CDN is mandatory), and custom domains may need
their own load balancer at ~$18-25/month (so Q8 is blocking).

FOUR OPERATING RULES FOR SURFACE CAPACITY (the Part 10 guardian rules have a
mirror here):
  1. Scale to zero unless someone paid not to. Always-on is a $30 SKU, not a
     default. This is the hosting equivalent of "never provision on hope."
  2. Sleep idle surfaces. Signal at 7 days idle, Prime at 30. A sleeping
     surface costs storage only. This is the equivalent of hibernating an
     idle dedicated guardian.
  3. Cache before you scale. Every static surface sits behind Cloud CDN from
     day one — at GCP's $0.12/GB origin egress, the CDN is not optional.
  4. Report COST PER SURFACE monthly, the way Part 10 reports cost per
     guardian. Two fixed costs, two utilisation reviews, one meeting.

A SURFACE COVERAGE NUMBER, analogous to the Guardian Coverage Ratio:
  Surface margin = (credits billed for that surface × $0.01 − actual GCP
  cost) ÷ credits billed. Target ≥ 65%. Warn < 50%. Act < 35% — either the
  surface is undersized for its traffic (move it to a metered plan) or we
  have mispriced its class.

THE PART 15 GATE THIS IMPLIES: no surface ever goes dark without two warnings
and a sleep state. This is guardrail #6 and it outranks the revenue.


PART 11 — BILLING FLOW AND MONEY PLUMBING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BILLING LIFECYCLE (a different rhythm than the credit flow — per cycle, in
months, not per action, in seconds):

  1. JEM identity — no HERE social profile required to hold a plan.
  2. Choose a plan in NOW.
  3. Gateway vaults the payment method — HERE never sees or stores card
     data (PCI stays with the gateway). If declined → dunning ladder below.
  4. Subscription + entitlement grant: credit allotment, Scircle seats,
     guardian lane, spend cap, retention window, queue priority — all
     granted together.
  5. Every billing cycle, one of:
       RENEW    — invoice, charge, reset the allotment. Unused plan credits
                  get a 30-day grace then expire. Purchased credits: 12mo.
       OVERAGE  — top-up, or auto-recharge only if the user opted in.
                  Minimum top-up $10 (see fee-math note below).
       CHANGE   — upgrade prorates immediately and credits land at once;
                  downgrade waits until the end of the current paid period.
       [v2] STANDING — Tier 8 hosting settles IN ARREARS at the end of each
                  cycle against the wallet. If the wallet is empty the app
                  does NOT fail: Studio and World draw overdraft; Signal and
                  Prime enter the warn → warn → sleep ladder from Part 6a. A
                  hosting charge is never the reason a site disappears
                  without warning.
  6. THE KEY ACCOUNTING RULE: cash in is NOT revenue. A credit purchase
     books to DEFERRED REVENUE (a liability) — the company now owes service.
  7. As credits BURN → recognise revenue, at the rate those specific
     credits were sold for.
  8. As credits EXPIRE unused → recognise BREAKAGE (free margin, but keep
     it under 8% of credit sales — see the North Star guardrails; high
     breakage looks like profit but is actually a sign of overselling).
  9. Report the unburned credit liability every month — it's the size of
     the service still owed, and it's the first number an investor or
     acquirer checks to see whether reported ARR is real.

Get steps 6-8 right from day one — retrofitting deferred-revenue accounting
later is a painful audit.

THE THREE MONEY-PLUMBING CALLS THE USER OWNS:

  GATEWAY:
    - Paddle (~5% + $0.50) — START HERE. Becomes the legal "merchant of
      record," handles worldwide VAT/GST/US sales tax automatically. A
      one- or few-person team cannot afford to self-manage global tax
      compliance.
    - Stripe Billing (2.9% + $0.30 + ~0.5-0.8%) — MOVE HERE once someone
      owns finance. Native subscriptions, usage meters, credit grants and
      entitlements — the closest off-the-shelf match to this exact
      two-part-tariff model. Best developer experience. But HERE becomes
      the seller of record and must register/remit tax itself (Stripe Tax
      calculates, does not file everywhere).
    - Razorpay (~2% domestic) — ADD if the billing entity is Indian. UPI
      AutoPay is, in practice, the only recurring-payment rail that works
      well there.
    - Keep the credit LEDGER (the immutable rows from Part 8) as HERE's own
      database, decoupled from whichever gateway is used via one adapter —
      never build the ledger on top of the gateway's own records, or
      switching gateways becomes a full data migration.

  CURRENCY:
    - Internal unit of account: USD (because all real costs — models,
      storage, egress — are billed in USD).
    - The ledger holds CREDITS ONLY, never currency. A euro customer and a
      rupee customer burn the identical credit — margin analysis needs zero
      FX conversion.
    - Sell at FIXED LOCAL PRICE POINTS (e.g. Prime: $39 / €39 / £33 /
      ₹2,999), re-priced quarterly — not live FX conversion, which produces
      prices like "€36.42" that read as careless.
    - Hold a 6-8% FX buffer in non-USD price points so a currency move gets
      absorbed at the next quarterly re-price instead of eating margin
      immediately.

  LEGAL SHAPE OF A CREDIT (not legal advice — a product-design shape, have
  a real lawyer confirm before the first payment):
    - Structure credits as NON-REFUNDABLE, NON-TRANSFERABLE, EXPIRING
      SERVICE USAGE RIGHTS. State this plainly on the buy screen, not just
      buried in the ToS.
    - NEVER: allow cash-out, peer-to-peer transfer, publish a "1 credit =
      1 cent, redeemable" guarantee, sell credits with no expiry, or make
      credits usable outside HERE. Each of these edges the product toward
      being regulated as a payments/e-money business, and toward
      unclaimed-property (escheatment) obligations in several US states.

  TOP-UP MINIMUM ($10) — not arbitrary, it's fee math: at 2.9% + $0.30, a
    $5 top-up costs $0.45 in fees (9%); a $2 top-up costs 18% (pure loss).
    A $10 floor plus pushing auto-recharge in $25/$50 blocks cuts the
    processing-fee line by roughly a third.

DUNNING LADDER (degrade, never delete — expect to recover 55-70% of failed
charges with this shape):
  Day 0        : charge fails. Nothing changes for the user yet.
  Days 1-7     : four retries (days 1, 3, 5, 7). One clear email each time
                 with a one-tap fix link. Full access holds throughout.
  Days 7-14    : GRACE — drops to the free lane. Reading/replay/export all
                 still work; paid jobs pause; guardians hibernate.
  Days 14-30   : SUSPENDED — read and export only. The Scircle stays intact
                 for everyone else in it. Still nothing deleted.
  Day 30       : cancelled. 60-day export window, then purge.


PART 12 — "EVERY ACTION IS A TOOL": THE MANIFEST ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This directly answers the user's stated architectural goal: wrap everything
as a HERE tool so it can be reskinned/remapped fast per project, with
premade templates letting search agents dynamically map UI ("a master DJ
setting the mood," "an on-demand harness"), and to enable an RSI
(recursive self-improvement) loop between a "prime guardian" and the HERE
tool chain.

THE KEY INSIGHT: if every billable thing is a tool, the rate card should not
be a spreadsheet someone maintains by hand — it should be a `meter` field
declared IN the tool template itself. A tool cannot ship without declaring
its meter, and the moment it does, it is automatically priced, quoted,
metered, held, settled, and reported. Zero incremental billing engineering
per new feature.

SHIP GATE (the rule that keeps this honest): a tool with NO `meter` block
ships as free, PERMANENTLY — not "unpriced," genuinely and visibly free.
This removes the temptation to ship something expensive now and price it
"later."

Example meter block (illustrative JSON shape for a tool template):
  {
    "tool": "capture.transcribe",
    "version": "3",
    "surface": "Main > Capture & stream",
    "meter": {
      "unit": "audio_minute",
      "rate": 3,                 // credits per unit
      "cogs": 0.006,              // USD per unit, our real cost
      "estimate": "duration_seconds / 60",
      "cap": 600,                 // refuse above this without a second GO
      "model_tier": null,         // or a model multiplier from Part 4
      "free_when": ["replay", "reread", "retry_after_our_failure"],
      "settle": "measured"        // never "estimated"
    },
    "go_card": {
      "headline": "Transcribe this recording",
      "shows": ["duration", "estimated_credits", "cap", "what_you_get"]
    }
  }

WHY THIS ENABLES THE RSI LOOP: every ledger row from Part 8 (person,
tool@version, model, units, credits, actual cost) flows back into the tool
template's own record. A "prime guardian" can then compare tool@version 3
against tool@version 4 on a real business metric — CREDITS PER SUCCESSFUL
OUTCOME — instead of only latency or a subjective rubric score. This is the
literal mechanism for "improving the code in a loop between prime guardian
and the HERE tool chain" that the user asked for.

THREE TOOL-PRICING TEMPLATE FAMILIES (kept to three so people — and search
agents mapping UI — can hold them in their head):

  INSTANT  — one unit, one price, under 2 seconds. Examples: room turn,
             Muse tool call, export, extraction page. No GO card shown —
             deducted silently, visible in the running total. Interrupting
             here would be worse than the charge itself.

  JOB      — measurable units, seconds to minutes. Examples: transcribe,
             render, guardian run, world recompute. Full GO card with an
             estimate and a cap. Hold → run → settle.

  STANDING — continuous or recurring. Examples: live production, scheduled
             guardians, storage, residual marketing from a broadcast. GO
             card must show a BUDGET AND A STOP CONDITION, metered per
             interval, auto-stops at the cap. NEVER open-ended by default —
             this directly enforces the separate product-direction memo's
             rule that "residual marketing... is not permission for
             indefinite unbounded activity" (see HERE-PROJECT-CONTEXT.txt
             §2).

PART 15 — SUGGESTED 90-DAY ROLLOUT (instrument → shadow-bill → charge)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The recommendation is NOT to launch a credit system live on day one — meter
first, shadow-bill second, charge third. The cost of a wrong rate card in
public is far higher than the cost of waiting six weeks.

  Days 1-15  : METER EVERYTHING, CHARGE NOTHING. Add the `meter` block
               (Part 12) to every tool. Write ledger rows. Build the wallet
               UI but keep it hidden.
               GATE: every billable surface writes a row, and the rows
               reconcile against real invoices within 5%.

  Days 16-30 : SHADOW BILLING. Nobody is charged. Internally compute what
               each account WOULD have paid under the Part 6 rate card.
               GATE: a median Prime-shaped account lands at 50-80% burn
               depth. If not, resize the allotment — never the prices.

  Days 31-45 : Signal and Prime go live. GO cards, holds, settle, receipts.
               Hard caps, NO overage yet — when the allotment runs out, it
               stops.
               GATE: zero charges on failed jobs; under 1 surprise report
               per 1,000 GOs.

  Days 46-60 : Top-ups and auto-recharge. $10 minimum. Auto-recharge
               strictly opt-in, with its own separate confirmation.
               GATE: waste below 2% of credits burned.

  Days 61-75 : Studio, the Dedicated Guardian add-on, and TIER 8 LIVE
               SURFACES go live. Priority lane, guardian-coverage
               reporting, pool rebalancing. On the hosting side: standing
               meters, renewal GO cards, the warn→warn→sleep ladder, the
               free envelope per plan, scale-to-zero defaults.
               GATE: accounts-per-guardian above 50 on every pool, GCR
               above 1.5× on each — AND no surface has gone dark without
               two warnings and a sleep state, and surface margin is above
               65% on every class. Do not ship Tier 8 at all until Q8 (the
               load-balancer question) is answered.

  Days 76-90 : World, annual plans, expiry and breakage all live. First
               formal margin review against the Part 3 metric tree.
               GATE: blended gross margin above 55% and climbing — THEN
               start on the Part 13 margin levers, not before.

THE ONE RULE THAT OUTRANKS EVERY REVENUE TARGET, THROUGH ALL 90 DAYS:
nobody is ever charged for something they did not knowingly start, and
nobody is ever charged for something that failed. Credits are a promise
that the system is being honest about what work costs. Every other number
in this plan is recoverable if it turns out wrong. That one is not.
