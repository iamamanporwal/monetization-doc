01

One-page summary

In plain words

A stablecoin is a digital dollar. One USDC is always worth about one US dollar. People can send it from a phone app to anyone in the world in seconds, for less than one cent.

We add "Pay with USDC" next to Card and UPI. The customer pays in digital dollars, Stripe instantly turns them into normal dollars, and those dollars land in our bank. The customer gets credits, exactly as if they had paid by card.

Why bother? Three reasons

1 · Cheaper1.5%

That is our fee on a $39 plan, compared with 3.7% for cards and 6.3% for Paddle. There is no fixed fee, so small payments stay cheap.

2 · More people can payNo card

This works for creators and builders whose cards fail on foreign SaaS, or who are already paid in USDC.

3 · Ready for agents24/7

Software can pay by itself, in tiny amounts. That is how Guardians and AI agents will buy work later.

The recommendation, answer by answer

Should we do this?Yes, as an extra option

USDC sits next to cards and UPI. It never replaces them.

Which coin?USDC only

It is the regulated one that merchants trust. Maybe EURC later.

Which blockchains?Solana + Base

Polygon is allowed too. Ethereum is allowed with a fee warning and hidden under $20.

Hold crypto?No. Convert 100%

The gateway turns every payment into dollars automatically.

Build or buy?Buy (Stripe first)

A payment gateway, not our own wallet.

Who is it for?Builders & creators

AI-native users, and people whose cards fail or cost extra.

How do we attract users?Bonus credits

A 5% / 3% / 2% bonus depending on plan. The price itself never drops.

What does it earn?+$0.35 to +$1.36

That is the extra margin per Prime payment, after paying the bonus, compared with card and with Paddle (Section 15).

What blocks us?Legal entity + platform

With a US entity this is simple. With an Indian entity it is complex (Section 10).

The one rule that outranks everything (from the Money Model)

Nobody is ever charged for something they did not knowingly start, and nobody is ever charged for something that failed. Paying in USDC must be exactly as safe and predictable as paying by card.

02

Plain-English glossary

In plain words

Crypto has a lot of jargon. Every word used in this document is explained here with an everyday comparison. If you are new to crypto, read this first.

The money itself

Stablecoin
A digital token designed to always be worth one unit of a normal currency, usually $1.a dollar bill that lives on the internet instead of in your pocket.
USDC
The stablecoin issued by Circle, a US company. It is backed 1:1 by cash and short-term US government bonds, and is regulated in the US and EU. This is the one we accept.the safe, boring, compliant digital dollar.
USDT (Tether)
The biggest stablecoin, issued offshore. It is popular in emerging markets but has regulatory problems in the EU and US.the popular dollar that many regulated shops won't take.
Fiat
Normal government money: US dollars, rupees, euros.the money in your bank account.
Depeg
When a stablecoin briefly stops being worth exactly $1, for example $0.97. This is rare for USDC, but it happened for a few days in March 2023.a dollar bill that trades at 97 cents for a weekend.
Tokenized deposit
A bank's own digital dollar, for example JPMorgan's JPMD. It works like a stablecoin but a bank issues it. Only institutions can use it for now.a stablecoin with a bank's name on it.

Moving it

Blockchain / chain / network
The public "road" the digital dollar travels on: Ethereum, Solana, Base, Polygon, Tron. The coin is the same on every road, but the tolls differ.motorways. Some have expensive tolls and some are almost free.
Wallet
The app where a person keeps digital dollars, such as MetaMask, Phantom or Coinbase Wallet.a banking app where you hold the keys yourself.
Self-custody
Holding your own wallet keys (passwords). If you lose them, the money is gone and no bank can reset it.cash in a safe that only you can open.
Gas / network fee
The toll paid to use the road. It is under one cent on Solana or Base. On Ethereum it is often under $1 but can spike above $20.a motorway toll that the sender pays.
Payment gateway
A company such as Stripe, Coinbase Commerce or CoinGate. It watches the chain, confirms that the payment arrived, screens it and converts it to normal money.a card terminal, but for digital dollars.
On-ramp / off-ramp
An on-ramp turns normal money into crypto. An off-ramp turns crypto back into normal money that can reach a bank.the currency exchange counter at an airport.
Settlement
The moment the money is final and belongs to us. Card payments take 2–3 days. Stablecoin payments take seconds.the cheque finally clearing.
Finality
The point after which a payment can't be reversed. On Solana that is about 1 second. On Base it is ~2 s "soft" and ~15 min "hard".the ink drying on a signature.
x402 / MPP
New protocols that let AI agents pay for things automatically over the internet using stablecoins. They are at an early stage.a tap-to-pay card for software.

Selling and the rules

Chargeback
A card customer asks their bank to take the money back, and the merchant loses the money plus a fee. Blockchain payments have no chargebacks.the bank reaching into our till.
Merchant of Record (MoR)
The company legally making the sale. Paddle is an MoR and handles global sales tax for us. Standard Stripe payments are not, so with Stripe we handle tax ourselves.the shop whose name is on the receipt.
KYC / KYB
"Know Your Customer" and "Know Your Business": identity checks that the law requires.showing ID to open an account.
AML
Anti-money-laundering rules, including screening wallets against sanctions lists.a bouncer checking the banned list.
GENIUS Act (US)
The US stablecoin law signed in July 2025. It regulates who may issue stablecoins and takes effect by 18 Jan 2027 at the latest.the rulebook for printing digital dollars.
MiCA (EU)
The EU's crypto law. It is the reason USDT disappeared from many EU checkouts.the EU's licence to operate.
VDA (India)
"Virtual Digital Asset", India's legal name for crypto, stablecoins included. Gains are taxed at 30% and transfers carry 1% TDS.the tax box that crypto falls into.
TDS (India)
Tax Deducted at Source: 1% withheld on crypto transfers under Section 194S.tax taken out before the money reaches you.

Accounting

FX / FX spread
Foreign exchange. The spread is the hidden cost between the "real" exchange rate and the rate you actually get.the gap between the rate on the news and the airport counter's rate.
Basis point (bps)
One hundredth of a percent, so 100 bps = 1%. It is used to quote small fees.cents on a $100 bill.
Deferred revenue
Money paid for service we have not delivered yet. It is something we still owe, not profit. It becomes revenue when credits are used.a gift card sitting in someone's drawer.
Breakage
Credits that were bought but expired unused.gift cards that never get spent.
03

The problem we are solving

In plain words

Today every payment into HERE loses a chunk to fees, some customers can't pay at all, and small top-ups lose money. Stablecoins fix part of all three problems.

Problem 1

Fees eat margin, most of all on small payments

Paddle charges ~5% + $0.50 and cards via Stripe ~2.9% + $0.30. The fixed part is what hurts small payments, and it is why the Money Model sets a $10 minimum top-up.

Problem 2

Some customers can't pay us easily

Many creators and builders outside the US and EU have cards that fail on international SaaS or add 3%+ in foreign fees. A growing group is paid in USDC by Deel, Meta or freelance platforms and would rather spend it directly.

Problem 3

The next customer may be an AI agent

Cards were built for humans typing numbers into forms. Stablecoins can be paid by software, 24/7, in tiny amounts. This is early, but it is where HERE's Guardians are heading.

What we are not trying to do

  • We are not trying to become a crypto company.
  • We are not replacing cards or UPI.
  • We will not let anyone cash credits out as crypto, because that would turn us into a regulated money business (Section 18).

In plain words

Think of sending a parcel. The customer (the sender) hands the parcel to a road (the blockchain). A courier (the gateway) watches the road, checks the parcel is right, and delivers it. A currency exchange (the off-ramp) swaps digital dollars for normal dollars, and the bank receives them.

06

Decision 1: Hold crypto or convert to dollars?

In plain words

When a customer pays in USDC, we can keep it as USDC or swap it into regular dollars straight away. Our bills (AI models, servers, Guardians) are all in dollars, so keeping USDC adds risk and paperwork for no benefit. We convert everything, automatically, every time.

nowOption A

Convert 100% automatically

Good: no crypto on our books, the same accounting as cards, no depeg risk, and simple for auditors and investors.

Bad: a small conversion cost, which Stripe already includes in its 1.5%.

noOption B

Hold 100% USDC

Good: we could pay suppliers and creators in USDC without converting again.

Bad: depeg risk, custody risk, crypto accounting, an Indian tax nightmare, and it scares banks and investors.

laterOption C

Convert by default + small USDC float

Good: the best of both.

Bad: it needs a written treasury policy and the discipline to follow it.

What the market does75.4% of CoinGate merchant payments convert to fiat, and the share keeping crypto has fallen three halves in a row.
Decision

Phases 0–5: Option A. Convert 100%.

After Phase 5: move to Option C only if we start paying Guardians, creators or suppliers in USDC (a separate Payouts PRD). The float is capped at 30 days of expected USDC payouts (Section 17). v1.1 fix

9.3The Paddle problem

The Money Model says "Paddle now, Stripe later", but Paddle has no crypto checkout. Stablecoins therefore force us onto one of three paths:

if US entityPath 1

Move billing to Stripe now

Cards and USDC in one place, with native meters and credits (Money Model page 4).

But: we take on global tax compliance (Stripe Tax), and it needs a US entity.

bridgePath 2

Paddle for cards + Coinbase Commerce for USDC

No migration needed.

But: two systems to reconcile, and we own the tax on USDC sales.

no gainPath 3

Keep Paddle, delay stablecoins

Zero work.

But: no margin gain and no access gain.

Worth checking before Path 1 · v1.1 additionStandard Stripe payments are not a Merchant of Record, but Stripe now also offers an MoR product (Stripe Managed Payments). Ask Stripe whether it supports stablecoin payments. If it does, we could keep Paddle-style tax coverage and get USDC in one system.

9.4Checklist: ask every gateway vendor

  • All-in cost on a $1,000 payment, with the FX spread in bps
  • Underpayment tolerance: is it configurable, and who covers the shortfall?
  • Wrong-network recovery: is it possible, what does it cost, how long does it take?
  • Late payment after the quote expires: what happens?
  • Rolling reserve: what %, for how long? (Some hold 5–10% for 30–180 days.)
  • Payout schedule and fees to our bank
  • Refund method: to the wallet? Partial refunds?
  • Recurring / subscription support
  • Is AML and sanctions screening included?
  • Countries where our entity is allowed
  • Accounting export: per-payment tx hash, rate and fee
  • Webhook reliability, retries, idempotency
  • Uptime history and status page
  • Does the vendor change supported coins without notice?

Design it

11

Users see ₹ or $, never "crypto"

In plain words

The customer thinks in rupees or dollars, and "USDC" is just a button like "UPI" or "Card". Behind the scenes we work out how many USDC equal the price, lock that amount for a few minutes, and handle all the conversion and bookkeeping. The customer never sees exchange rates, blockchains or jargon unless they want to.

PLAN

Prime

₹2,999 / month

or $39 · €39 · £33

5,000 credits every month

PAY WITH

CardUPIUSDC+5% credits
  1. PRICELook up the fixed local price from the quarterly price table ($39 / €39 / £33 / ₹2,999). Never use live FX.
  2. QUOTEConvert it to a USD amount for the USDC path, using our price table.
  3. LOCKLock that USDC amount for 30 minutes.
  4. PRESENTShow the exact USDC amount, the network and the estimated gas.
  5. DETECTThe gateway watches the chain and matches the payment to the invoice.
  6. SETTLEThe gateway converts USDC → USD and sends a "paid" webhook.
  7. GRANTThe entitlement service grants the credits.
  8. RECORDThe ledger writes one immutable row (Section 14).

Six rules

R1

Credits are the only unit inside HERE. Card, UPI and USDC buyers get the same credits for the same plan.

R2

The ledger never holds currency or crypto. (Money Model rule.)

R3

Never show a live rate like "36.42 USDC". Quotes are whole cents from our price table.

R4

Gas is shown before signing. There are no surprise costs.

R5

The word "crypto" appears only on the USDC payment screen and its help page, nowhere else.

R6

Stripe presents in USD (other currencies are in private preview). Showing the local currency is our job, via step 2.

New open question · v1.1If a customer sees a regional price (₹2,999) and pays in USDC, the draft quotes $36.00, which is cheaper than the $39 US price. That implies ~₹83/$, so check it against the current quarterly table. It also creates an arbitrage: anyone who can appear to be in India pays $3 less. Decide whether USDC always quotes the USD list price, or honours regional prices only for verified regions.

User flows

In plain words

These are the exact steps a customer goes through. We start with the simplest one, a one-off top-up, and add subscriptions once top-ups work.

AOne-off top-up build first

  1. In NOW, the user taps Add credits and picks $10 / $25 / $50 / custom.
  2. They choose USDC. (Card and UPI keep their existing flows.)
  3. The quote screen shows what they pay, what they get, the network and a 30-minute timer.
  4. Stripe's hosted crypto page opens, and the user connects Phantom, Coinbase Wallet or MetaMask.
  5. The user signs one transaction, and the chain confirms in ~1–2 s.
  6. Stripe's "paid" webhook fires, and HERE grants the credits to the Purchased bucket.
  7. The receipt reads: "2,625 credits added. Tx: 5Xk…9aP".

16.3Chargebacks: card vs USDC

Card

The bank decides

The customer complains to their bank, and the bank pulls the money back. We lose the money plus a dispute fee, and have to send evidence.

USDC

We decide, using our policy

The customer complains to us. Nobody can pull money back without us. The risk shifts from "fraud reversal" to "our refund policy".

17

Treasury policy: what we do with the money

In plain words

Treasury means how a company manages its cash. Our policy is simple: turn every digital dollar into a real dollar immediately. The only exception is a small, capped pot of USDC, kept later for paying people abroad in USDC.

T1 · Default

100% auto-conversion to USD at the gateway.

T2 · Exposure

Seconds to minutes between payment and conversion, which is effectively zero.

T3 · Float (only once USDC payouts exist)

Max 30 days of expected payouts, USDC only, held at a regulated custodian (never self-custody), and reviewed monthly.

T4 · No yield chasing

No lending, staking or "earn" products. (GENIUS bans issuers from paying interest. Third-party "rewards" exist, but we don't use them.)

T5 · Kill switch

If USDC trades below $0.98 for more than 1 hour, pause USDC checkout and payouts until it recovers.

T6 · Reporting

A monthly report of USDC received, converted, fees, float balance and incidents.

Money flow with a float (later phase)

Customers pay USDC→ Gateway100% converted→ USD bank→ USDC float≤ 30 days · payouts only→ Guardians / creators
18

Tax, legal and compliance

In plain words

Accepting digital dollars is legal in most places, but each country has its own rules. There are two big risks: (1) accidentally becoming a "money business" that needs licences, and (2) tax treatment in India. A one-hour lawyer and accountant review before launch avoids both.

18.1Credits are still not money

AllowedUSDC → credits

The customer pays in with USDC and receives credits.

Forbiddencredits ✕→ USDC

Cashing credits out, sending credits between users for value, or promising credits can be redeemed for USDC. Each one pushes us toward money-transmitter regulation.

18.2 · United States

Simple

The GENIUS Act (July 2025) regulates stablecoin issuers, not merchants. It takes effect on 18 Jan 2027 or 120 days after final rules, whichever is earlier. The Federal Reserve published its proposed rule on 29 Sep 2026.

Our duties are normal sales tax (Stripe Tax) and income tax. Stripe screens wallets. LEGAL Confirm the credit terms and USDC acceptance with a US lawyer.

18.3 · European Union

Medium

MiCA is in force, so use only MiCA-compliant coins (USDC, EURC). VAT on digital services still applies by customer country.

Leo is in Prague. If an EU entity bills, use a MiCA-licensed gateway.

18.4 · India TAX LEGAL

Complex

Stablecoins are VDAs (s. 2(47A)): a 30% flat tax on gains (115BBH) and 1% TDS on transfers (194S). Converting USDC to INR is a taxable transfer. Providers must be FIU-IND registered, and CARF reporting starts in 2026.

The compliant route is to convert offshore and remit INR with an eFIRA. Indian customers who pay in USDC take on their own TDS, so UPI is the default for Indian users.

18.5Compliance checklist before launch

  • Lawyer review of the credit terms and USDC acceptance (in the billing country)
  • CA review if any Indian entity touches crypto
  • Gateway contract: AML and sanctions screening confirmed
  • Terms of service: USDC refunds go only to the sending wallet
  • Privacy policy: wallet addresses are personal data
  • Tax engine configured for USDC sales (Stripe Tax)
  • Accounting: USDC receipts booked as deferred revenue

vs card: saved $0.65, gave back ≤ $0.25 → kept +$0.40
Example 2

An underpayment on Prime

  1. Invoice: 39.00 USDC.
  2. The customer withdraws from an exchange that deducts a $0.30 fee, so 38.70 USDC arrives.
  3. The shortfall of $0.30 is under the $0.50 tolerance, so it is treated as paid in full.
Cost to us: $0.30. A support ticket costs far more, so this is the right call.
Example 3

Why the World bonus is 2%, not 5%

  1. World: $999, 175,000 credits/month.
  2. Card fee $29.27, USDC fee $14.99, so the saving is $14.29.

An Indian user sees an Indian price

  2. If they pick USDC, the system quotes $36.00 from the price table, i.e. 36.00 USDC (see open question 8).

This is why UPI is the default for Indian users.
